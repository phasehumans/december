paul graham has a new essay, [_making startups powerful_](https://www.paulgraham.com/powerful.html). the question he asks founders is not "how do you make more money?" it's "what would make this company more powerful?" the money question gets you small wins. the power question sometimes gets you something ten times bigger.

he gives a lot of ways to answer it. own the customer, not just a part they buy. let the money flow through you. find network effects where nobody expects them. go full stack: do the job yourself instead of selling tools to the people who do it. sell to startups early, because they decide fast and grow with you. give things away, through open source or an api, because generous companies end up with a small slice of a much bigger pie. and watch for users "misusing" your product. paypal started as a demo for security software, until ebay sellers began using it to get paid.

the part i liked most is the catch at the end. none of this works unless it makes things better for the customer. a new startup is too weak to force anything on anyone, so the only way it gets powerful is by making someone's life better. and often the thing holding a startup back is not another company. it's the founders, doing something smaller than they could because they're scared. a lot of his advice comes down to one word: just.
