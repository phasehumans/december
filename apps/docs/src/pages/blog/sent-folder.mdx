for most of history, when someone was gone, almost nothing of their thinking stayed. a few letters, some stories that changed a little every time they were told. we're different, mostly by accident. we write all day: messages, emails, notes, voice notes. without trying, we leave a record of how we think, what we find funny, and how we make up our minds. it's the biggest record of ordinary human thought there has ever been, and most of it is scattered across apps we'll stop using and phones we'll lose.

the models that come later will read that record far better than today's can. not to pretend to be the person, but so you could ask, what would they have thought about this? and get an answer in their own words, from something they actually wrote. people have always lived on in the memories of others, and memories fade. a record doesn't have to. if we keep it on purpose, the people we lose can leave more than photos. they can leave how they thought.
