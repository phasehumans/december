paul graham has an essay called [_copy what you like_](https://www.paulgraham.com/copy.html). the point is that most of us copy what impresses us, not what we actually like, and those are not the same thing. impressive is what other people admire. liked is what you go back to when nobody is watching. his test is simple: take away the name and the frame. if you found the painting at a garage sale, dirty, with no painter on it, would you still want it? or look at what you read when you are not trying to be a better person. that is your real taste.

it works for software too. we copy the stack the big company uses, the architecture from the famous blog post, the design everyone shares, because it looks serious. but the tools you actually reach for at 2am are usually the boring ones. copy those. and when you copy something that is really good, copy the part that makes it good, not the part that is easy to see. the flaws are always easier to copy.
